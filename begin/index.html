Begin your journey

You know there's more

You've probably had the pay rise that changed nothing. Or the bonus that turned something you liked into a chore. Not ingratitude, and not just you — it happens reliably enough that people measured it. Here it is. Have a go.

So the question isn't pay. It's what to protect. Three things keep anyone going at something hard: choosing how you do it, feeling yourself get better, and it mattering to somebody. Lose one and the work goes flat. Hence a little further each day — better is a direction, not a place you arrive.

It isn't a one-off, and the talk it comes from is worth your time:

  • Replicated over and over, by psychologists, sociologists and economists.
  • Pay someone too little and none of it applies. The point of paying properly is to get money out of the way.
  • People built Linux, Apache and Wikipedia in their own time, for nothing. Atlassian gives its engineers a day a quarter and ships what comes out of it.

That gets you as far as one person goes. What you'd start tomorrow if you had the room is bigger than that — so the ceiling isn't what you can do, it's how much of what's in your head somebody else can pick up and carry. Three things you bring, over the one that decides how much gets out:

agency = ( perspective + worldview + culture ) ÷ values

  • Perspective — your credibility: maturity, and whether you show up competitive or cooperative.
  • Worldview — your ordering principle, your plan for interacting, your reliability.
  • Culture — intimacy, vibe, and the stock of common knowledge you take for granted.
  • Values — the divisor, and the one you can move: your filter, your self-orientation, how you like to listen, how you speak.

The top line took you years and won't shift much this week. The bottom line you could change by Friday — and it is the one that decides the total.

None of it is about how much you can hold in your head. It's about moving people and being moved — most of what work turns out to be. The three qualities that make someone good at it land on the same four terms:

  • Attunement — getting into someone else's view of it. That is perspective, renamed.
  • Buoyancy — staying afloat in an ocean of no. What predicts it is how you explain the last one to yourself: worldview. Questioning your own ability beforehand beats pumping yourself up.
  • Clarity — telling signal from noise. Access to information stopped being an advantage; sorting it is one. That's the filter, in values.
  • And where it lands — the common ground where you both come out better off — is culture.

So point the trust equation at yourself, not at everybody else. Credibility plus reliability plus intimacy over self-orientation — it lines up with those four term for term, self-orientation inside values, the divisor in both. Run it inward until you're someone whose judgement you'd back.

Then the thing you wanted stops being a solo project. That's the "more" you can already feel — it was never coming from working harder on your own.

Why some things stay out of reach

There are things you would do if you knew how. Not because they are mysterious — because learning enough to start costs more than the job is worth, so you go on doing without.

Whatever did not move was never a question. It was a choice — and choices do not get easier when you know more.
