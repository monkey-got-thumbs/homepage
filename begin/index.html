Begin your journey

You know there's more

Worth taking seriously, because it's the only fuel that works here. Past the point where money stops being the problem, three things do the work: directing your own effort, getting better at something, and doing something that matters.

Which is why the promise out front is a little further each day. Getting better is a direction, not a destination — the pull comes from moving, not arriving. One thing you couldn't do last week is the whole mechanism.

The twelve-minute animation of the research is worth your time. What it lands:

  • Pay too little and nothing else works. Pay fairly to take money off the table, so the work can do the motivating.
  • What's left over: self-direction, getting better, purpose. Not sentiment — the thing that predicts good work.
  • Skilled people built Linux, Apache and Wikipedia in their own time, for nothing.
  • Atlassian gives its engineers a day a quarter on anything they like, and ships what comes out.

The reach itself comes from other people, and collaboration is a question of agency. Three things you bring, over the one that decides how much of them gets out:

agency = ( perspective + worldview + culture ) ÷ values

  • Perspective — your credibility: maturity, and whether you show up competitive or cooperative.
  • Worldview — your ordering principle, your plan for interacting, your reliability.
  • Culture — intimacy, vibe, and the stock of common knowledge you take for granted.
  • Values — the divisor, and the term you can move: your filter, your self-orientation, how you like to listen, how you speak.

Notice what's missing. None of it is about capacity, or how much you can hold at once. It's about moving people and being moved — which is most work now, whoever you are. The three qualities that make someone good at it land on the same terms:

  • Attunement is perspective-taking — tuning yourself to someone else. That is perspective, renamed.
  • Buoyancy is staying afloat in an ocean of rejection. What predicts it is how you explain failure afterwards — an ordering principle, so worldview. Questioning your ability beforehand beats pumping yourself up.
  • Clarity is telling signal from noise. Access to information stopped being an advantage; curating it is one. That's the filter, in values.
  • And where it ends — the common ground where you're both better off — is culture.

Which is where trust comes in, usually pointed the wrong way. Credibility plus reliability plus intimacy, over self-orientation — used as a scorecard for other people. It isn't one. Set it beside the four above and it lines up term for term and in the same positions — self-orientation inside values, the divisor in both.

So it is an instrument for auditing your own agency. You run it inward, on your own three-over-one, and you work on the divisor — because that's the term that moves.

Which is why the three together beat any one alone. Motivation gives you the reason to reach. Trusting your own agency lets you hand something over without controlling it. Then someone else's agency stops being an obstacle and becomes what carries the idea further than you could. That's the "more" you can already feel — it was never coming from working harder on your own.

The motivation research is Daniel Pink's — Drive (2009), as presented in his RSA lecture; the reward experiment in the figure is the Madurai replication he describes there, from Ariely, Gneezy, Loewenstein and Mazar's Large Stakes and Big Mistakes. Attunement, buoyancy and clarity are his too, from To Sell Is Human (2012) and the RSA short drawn from it — where the point about explanatory style predicting resilience is credited to Martin Seligman at the University of Pennsylvania. The way of picturing trust is David Maister, Charles Green and Robert Galford's, from The Trusted Advisor.
